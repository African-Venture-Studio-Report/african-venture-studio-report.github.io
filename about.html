Foreword from the authors

We set out to answer a question the published record leaves open. How many companies in African venture studio portfolios secure external investment, and who writes their first cheque? Existing research reports that studio companies reach early funding sooner and raise follow-on capital more often than companies supported by other models, but those rates describe specific groups, such as the companies studios built in-house or those that have already raised a seed round.

We approached the question from within the model, having built and supported ventures in Kenyan venture studios. That experience shaped the question, and the evidence decides the answers. Our associations with three studios in the sample, and the safeguards applied, are set out in Annex A.7.

We verified 68 African venture studios, documented their publicly disclosed portfolios and matched 456 companies to funding records from January 2019 to September 2026. That let us examine fundraising outcomes, identify the investors involved and compare studio companies with startups outside the model.

Two findings stand out. First, just over a quarter of companies in the disclosed portfolios, 124 of 456, have secured a recorded external round, which makes the first external cheque into studio companies the largest opportunity for investors and funders.

Second, studio companies that secure a first round are more likely to raise again. Among companies first funded between January 2021 and September 2024, 48% of studio companies raised a second round within 24 months, against 28% of startups outside studios, an advantage concentrated among studios that run their own funds. Most of the funding gap therefore occurs before the first external cheque.

This report is a starting point. Despite our checks, omissions and classification errors are possible, and we welcome corrections from practitioners. We offer the findings to investors, development finance institutions, studio operators and policymakers shaping how studios are structured, financed and evaluated across Africa, and to researchers pursuing the questions in Chapter 9.

We thank the studio practitioners and contributors who shared their time, insights and data. We owe a particular debt to Briter, whose white paper with FMO and GIZ SAIS and whose African Venture Studio Summit Directory did the groundwork of identifying and profiling the continent’s studios, giving this report the foundation it builds on. We are grateful to Maxime Bayen, co-founder of Africa: The Big Deal, for the funding data at the heart of this research and for permission to use it, and to Cindy Ai and Mark Kleyner of Dream VC for their support throughout.

We hope you enjoy reading the report and find it useful in your work.
