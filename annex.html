A. Data, matching, statistical methods and limitations

A.1 Data

Round and investor data come from Africa: The Big Deal, covering 1 January 2019 to 30 September 2026, and are used with their permission. The report refers to them as the deal record. After one duplicate was removed, the deals database holds 4,678 announced transactions, and the investors database holds 2,625 investors. The source records rounds of $1 million and above in 2019, $500,000 and above in 2020 and $100,000 and above from 2021. Amounts are nominal US dollars as recorded, without inflation adjustment. Name variants of the same institution are merged, and placeholder or unidentified names are excluded from investor counts. The studio directory and portfolio records were compiled and verified in October 2026, and all calculations are held in the analysis workbook dated 16 October 2026.

A.2 How the 464 portfolio entries were built

  1. Each company a studio listed publicly between August and October 2026, on its website or in published disclosures, is one portfolio entry. Companies a studio has stopped listing fall outside the denominator, which may raise the headline rate (Exhibit 42 reports both.) The 68 studios list 464 portfolio entries, and the 19 studios that list no company stay in the studio count.

  2. Company names were standardised before matching. Case, punctuation and bracketed former names were ignored, and an alias table links renamed companies. No studio lists a company twice, and eight companies appear in two studios’ portfolios, so the 464 entries resolve to 456 companies.

  3. Each company was matched to the deal record by name and confirmed by website where both sources give one. Six matches with differing websites were reviewed individually, and records sharing a name but belonging to a different business were rejected. One of the six could not be verified, so it counts as a company with no recorded external round. Accepting the match would raise the headline rate to 27.4 percent (Exhibit 12).

  4. A company is counted as funded if at least one matched round names an outside investor (Annex A.3). Its rounds, capital and investors are attached to the company once, and to each of its portfolio entries where a comparison by studio needs them.

A.3 Counting rules

  1. Analyses of companies use the 456 companies, and comparisons between studios use the 464 portfolio entries. The headline rate, the round funnel, the investor analysis and the comparison with startups outside studios count companies. Comparisons by structure, region, country and Briter listing count entries. Median funding rates by studio cover studios with three or more disclosed companies.

  2. A round is external if it names at least one investor other than the studio or its own fund. Rounds funded only by the studio or its fund are excluded, and unnamed angels alongside the studio do not make a round external. Debt and grants from an outside party count as external rounds, though not as equity. A round naming no investor stays external unless something indicates studio funding. The 456 companies have 332 recorded rounds other than acquisitions. The studio alone funded 16, leaving 316 external rounds, of which 303 name an outside investor and 13 name none. Because a company counts as funded only if a round names an outside investor, the 124 funded companies do not rely on the 13 unnamed rounds. Only the studio and its own fund are treated as affiliated. Two investors with published links to one studio count as outside investors, and treating both as affiliated would lower the headline rate to 26.3 percent (Exhibit 12).

  3. Mergers and acquisitions are counted separately as exits and excluded from funding rounds. No studio company has a recorded initial public offering.

  4. Undisclosed amounts are treated as missing and excluded from capital totals.

  5. Time to first external round covers companies launched in 2019 or later, so that earlier rounds outside the record are not missed. Launch year comes from the deal record and exists only for companies with a recorded round. The second-round comparison uses first rounds from January 2021 to September 2024. The window opens once the source’s threshold settled at $100,000 and closes early enough to give every company 24 months of record afterwards. All other analysis uses the full record from January 2019 to September 2026.

  6. Investor participations count named investors only. A later round enters the continuity measure in Chapter 4 if it and an earlier round of the same company each name an investor. An investor returns when the same standardised name appears in the later round and in any earlier round, ordered by date.

  7. Funding rates are not reported for groups with fewer than ten observations, and counts are given instead. The stage shares in Exhibit 24 are descriptive and shown for every investor profiled, except the two studio vehicles.

  8. A first-round lead is recorded only where a published announcement of the round states that an investor led or co-led it. No lead is inferred from cheque size, order of mention or seniority. Investor and lead analyses use each funded company’s first external round that names an investor.

A.4 Statistical methods

Every figure comes from our analysis workbook dated 16 October 2026. We rebuilt the analysis separately, repeating the name matching between the portfolio lists and the deal record, and reproduced every reported count. The clustered and permutation tests come from that rebuild, which also reconstructs the comparator group for the second round comparison, within five companies of the 1,612.

Confidence intervals on proportions are 95 percent Wilson intervals. The second round comparison uses a Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio stratified by region and first round year, with Robins, Breslow and Greenland confidence intervals and a Mantel-Haenszel test with continuity correction. The comparison by studio structure uses a Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio stratified by region, alongside a Mann-Whitney test of funding rates by studio. Other comparisons between two groups use Fisher’s exact test, and the comparison across four regions a chi-square test. An odds ratio compares odds, not probabilities, so an odds ratio of 2.21 does not mean a group is 2.21 times as likely to raise.

Units and clustering. Tests on companies count each company once, and tests on portfolio entries count a company once for each studio that lists it. Entries cluster within studios, as do studio companies in the second-round comparison. Every principal clustered test was therefore checked at studio level, with standard errors clustered by studio and, for portfolio entries, a permutation test that reassigns labels across whole studios. Exhibit 40 reports both.

Multiple testing. The Bonferroni correction covers 15 tests, made up of the ten findings in Exhibit 40, three further specifications in Exhibit 14 (sector, early stage and launch since 2019) and the two structure subgroups in Exhibit 15. Clustered regressions and permutation tests of the same hypothesis are not counted separately, while the rank test of studio funding rates examines a different hypothesis and is counted. At the corrected threshold of 0.0033 (0.05 divided by 15), only the difference by studio structure holds once clustering within studios is accounted for, with p < 0.001 in both the clustered regression and the permutation test. Exhibit 40 reports the result for every other finding. A result that is not significant does not show that no difference exists.

A.6 Limitations

A.7 Author interests

The authors have worked within or alongside venture studios in Kenya, and three of those studios appear in the sample. Records for these studios were coded and verified within the author team, so three safeguards apply. First, all three studios are named in both of Briter’s publications, so their inclusion did not depend on the authors’ judgement, and the same inclusion and classification rules applied to them as to every other studio. Second, their funding records come from the third-party deal record, independent of the studios themselves. Third, an independent rebuild repeated the name matching from the portfolio lists and the deal record and reproduced every reported count, which confirms the figures are internally consistent but does not verify the underlying records. None of the three studios reviewed this report before publication or saw any figure concerning another studio. Together these measures limit the risk of bias, though they do not amount to external verification.

Annex E. Glossary and abbreviations

E.1 Terms

Term Meaning in this report
Approach How far the evidence supports an implication in Chapter 8: Ready now, Pilot or Test first (Exhibit 38).
Build route Whether a studio built a company, co-created it with founders, or invested in a company it did not build. Not yet recorded for every company.
Capital recycling The return of capital to a studio through exits and share sales in later rounds, so it can be reinvested in new companies (Chapter 1).
Clustering Companies and portfolio entries at the same studio are not independent observations. Clustered tests treat the studio as the unit of analysis.
Companies The 456 companies in the disclosed portfolios, each counted once. Company-level figures use this unit. Other figures count portfolio entries, rounds, investor participations or investors, and each exhibit note states its unit.
Dual entity studio A studio that separates company building from an investment fund, either managed internally (internal fund) or governed separately (sidecar fund).
Evidence grades Strong rests on the full sample. Qualified is a strong association whose cause is unresolved, or whose significance depends on a subgroup or the unit of analysis. Indicative rests on small numbers, interview evidence, published reporting alone, or a result that does not survive correction for multiple testing. Untested means no data yet, with interview or published evidence only.
External round A recorded round, other than a merger or acquisition, that names at least one investor other than the company’s studio or the studio’s own fund, or names no investor at all. Rounds funded only by the studio are excluded (Annex A.3).
First external round The first external round in the deal record from January 2019. A company that raised before then may have had earlier outside capital.
Funded companies and entries The 124 companies that have raised externally, and the 132 portfolio entries they form.
Funding rate The share of companies, or of portfolio entries, that have raised externally.
Investor participation One named investor present in one external round.
Lead investor An investor that a published announcement of the round states led or co-led it. No lead is inferred from cheque size, order of mention or seniority (Annex A.3).
Other structures Hybrid studios also sell services or run accelerator programmes, corporate embedded studios sit within a larger company, donor backed studios are backed by a donor or foundation, and unclassified studios could not be assigned.
Portfolio entries One company in one studio’s published portfolio. The eight companies held by two studios count once for each, giving 464 entries, used only to compare groups of studios.
Raised externally Has at least one external round since January 2019 that names an investor other than the company’s studio or the studio’s own fund. Only these companies count as funded.
Region The studio’s regional cluster by headquarters (West, East, Southern or North Africa), with Angola grouped with Southern Africa.
Single entity studio A studio that builds and invests from one holding company.
Startups outside studios Companies in the same deal record with no connection to any of the 68 studios.
Studio companies The companies in the disclosed portfolios of the 49 studios that publish one, whether the studio built, co-created or invested in them.
Studios with their own funds Shorthand for dual entity studios with an internal fund. The one sidecar fund studio is reported separately.
Venture studio An organization that builds companies to investment readiness, recruits co-founders to run them, provides an in-house operating team, invests its own cash and takes equity in return. These are the five criteria published by FMO, GIZ SAIS and Briter (2026), set out in Exhibit 4. Shortened to studio in this report.
