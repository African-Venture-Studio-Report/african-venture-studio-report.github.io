Chapter Eight

Make first rounds measurable, then pilot what lifts them

The largest gap is at entry. Portfolio funding rates differ most from the published benchmark at the first external round, and the advantage over other startups appears only after it, mainly at studios with their own funds. The evidence locates the problem more firmly than it identifies the remedy, so each implication in Exhibit 38 carries one of three labels. Ready now marks actions the findings support directly. Pilot marks actions where the findings establish the gap but not the remedy. Test first marks actions whose underlying proposition is untested.

1

Funders should ask studios to report how many companies reach a first external round

A follow-on rate describes companies that investors have already selected. Development finance’s share of commitments to African venture funds is falling (Chapter 1), so funders need clearer evidence of how studio companies reach external capital. The share of all portfolio companies reaching a first external round within a defined window is the earliest milestone on the route to the exits and share sales that recycle capital (Chapter 1), and it varies widely across studios. Funders who require it would also create the dataset this report assembled from public sources. The evidence does not show that first round co-investment would lift that share, nor how often first rounds lack a lead. Co-investment committed only alongside an independent outside investor is meant to limit the risk of funding companies no one else would back, though it cannot guarantee independent underwriting. Such co-investment should be piloted against comparable studios, matched on structure, sector and company vintage, with the first-round funding rate across each full portfolio as the measure, before it is scaled.

2

Studio founders should design the first round into the studio model

A studio should know before it builds how each company will reach its first outside investor. The structural finding is an association, and it does not prescribe a fund for every studio. McKinsey reports fewer first-time private equity funds in 2025 than at any point in the past decade, and the white paper warns that dual entity studios risk drifting into conventional venture investing.35 A committed partner fund, as in one Côte d’Ivoire studio’s partnership with Ring Capital, may therefore offer much of the benefit of a studio’s own fund, though its cost and outcomes have not been tested. A published ceiling on the studio’s stake, with defined terms for reducing it as the founders take over, could turn an argument in diligence into a term the investor has already priced, though its effect is untested.

3

Investors gain a first cheque channel, but follow-on depends on studio funds

Investors drawing on studio portfolios meet few repeat investors at the first round and see companies that have passed a studio’s selection. The second-round advantage sits with studios that have their own funds, while companies of single entity studios progress at the rate of other startups. Investors should therefore underwrite the studio’s capacity to follow on, and each company’s build route, as carefully as the company itself. Ownership and financing risks may differ between companies a studio built, co-created or invested in.

4

Policymakers and industry bodies should make the denominator visible

Information is the most important public good in this market. A disclosure standard agreed among studios and their funders, listing every company, its launch year, build route and status, would make the funding rate across whole portfolios easier to calculate and extend benchmarks beyond the studios that can be documented today. In North Africa, the next step is to test whether cross-border investment and exit rules constrain financing, before any new early-stage programme or reform is designed.

A reporting standard that covers every portfolio company has the strongest evidence behind it, and it would also produce the data the pilots and tests depend on. Chapter 9 sets out the questions that data needs to answer.
