The first external cheque

Nearly three in four companies in disclosed African venture studio portfolios have no recorded external round. The gap to the published benchmark sits at that first step, and the advantage over other startups appears only once companies have raised.

01

Most of the gap in funding rates sits at the first external round

27% of disclosed portfolio companies have a recorded external round, against 40% or 49% for in-house studio companies in the published benchmark. The gap narrows at each later round.

Chapter 3 · Exhibits 11 and 13
02

Follow-on funding rates are higher, but concentrated in studios with their own funds

48% of studio companies first funded in 2021 to 2024 raised again within 24 months, against 28% of other startups. At studios with their own funds the rate is 69%.

Chapter 3 · Exhibits 14 and 15
03

Investor participation is fragmented, and African investors fall away at later stages

71% of first round investors joined only one. African investors hold 59% of named participations at pre-seed and 4% at Series C and later.

Chapter 4 · Exhibits 22 and 23
04

Portfolio companies at studios with their own funds reach a first external round three times as often

52% of portfolio companies at studios with their own funds have a first external round, against 17% at single entity studios. An association, not yet a cause.

Chapter 5 · Exhibits 29 and 30
05

More visible studios have higher recorded funding rates

Companies at the 41 studios in published directories reach an external round at 34%, against 13% at the 27 studios added by this study.

Chapter 5 · Exhibit 32
06

International studio benchmarks cannot yet be confirmed on African data

Fewer than one in five seed funded studio companies reach Series A, in line with other startups and far below the 72% cited internationally.

The headline rate is robust. What drives differences between studios remains open.

The share of portfolio companies with a recorded external round stays between 26% and 29% under every definition tested. The follow-on advantage holds across regions, years, sectors and stages, but leans on the largest portfolio. Studios’ own funds show the firmest link to higher funding rates, though it is not yet clear whether these studios build stronger companies or back them. Exhibit 41 grades every headline figure.

Strategic implications

Eight actions for four groups of decision makers

The most important: studios publish funding outcomes for every company they hold, funders concentrate support on the first external round, and venture investors treat studio portfolios as a regular source of first deals.

1

Require studios to report the share of all portfolio companies that reach a first external round

Alongside follow-on rates.

2

Pilot co-investment at the first external round alongside a committed independent investor

Scale it only if first round rates rise.

3

Design each company’s route to its first external investor before building it

Whether through an internal fund, a committed partner vehicle or an agreed syndicate.

4

Publish standard equity terms, including a ceiling on the studio’s stake that falls as founders take over

Investors weigh founder ownership closely, though the data cannot yet show whether a ceiling lifts first round rates.

5

Build repeat relationships with studios as a source of first deals

Most investors in studio companies’ first rounds (71%) have joined only one, so few have yet built a pipeline of first deals from studio portfolios.

6

Assess the studio’s ability to fund later rounds as closely as the company itself

The second-round advantage sits with studios that have their own funds, while companies of studios without a fund raise again at about the same rate as startups outside studios (29% against 28%).

7

Establish a common disclosure standard with studios, DFIs and LPs

Covering every portfolio company’s launch year, build route and status, updated each year.

8

Assess whether cross-border capital and exit rules are holding back fundraising in North Africa

North African studio portfolios have the lowest recorded funding rate of the four regions, at 17%.
